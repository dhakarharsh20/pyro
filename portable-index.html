When to choose a two-colour model P250C and P450C explained +

The problem two-colour solves

Standard pyrometers measure radiation at one wavelength and assume a set emissivity. If emissivity is wrong — because of scale, variable surface condition, or partial steam between you and the target — the reading is wrong. A two-colour (ratio) pyrometer measures at two adjacent wavelengths and divides them. Because both wavelengths are affected proportionally by emissivity variation or partial obscuration, the ratio cancels the error.

When P250C or P450C is the right choice

  • Surface emissivity is unknown or variable — oxidised metals, scale, changing surface conditions
  • Steam, smoke, or dust partially obscures the line of sight
  • You're measuring through a glass window with unknown transmission loss
  • Rolling mill inspection — surface condition changes continuously between passes

If emissivity is stable and the sightline is clear, a standard P-Series gives better resolution. Two-colour is for variable or unknown emissivity — not every situation.

Through-lens optical sighting — P Series What it means for accuracy +

Through-lens (TTL) sighting means you look through the same optical path the pyrometer uses to measure. The crosshair is exactly on the measurement spot. This matters when targeting small objects at distance, tracking moving targets, or working in conditions where misalignment by even a few degrees changes what you're measuring. TI series uses a reflex or electronic viewfinder — adequate for large targets at close range. For small targets, long distances, or moving objects, TTL sighting in the P Series gives you the certainty that the reading is on the right spot. The P Series also includes dioptry correction (−2.5 to +2.5) so operators with different vision can use the instrument without glasses.

Frequently asked questions 3 questions before ordering +
What is the maximum temperature these can measure? +
3,000°C on the P450 (700°C – 3,000°C sub-range). For most foundry and steelworks applications below 2,500°C, the P250 or P450C covers the range with better accuracy and a lower cost.
Can portable pyrometers measure through glass or steam? +
Yes — but only specific models. TI 1800, P250, P450, P250C, and P450C are designed for this. TI 1500 and P390 are not. The spectral range of each model determines whether it can see through the medium. Confirm the specific medium before ordering — glass and steam require different spectral approaches.
How does portable accuracy compare to fixed-mount pyrometers? +
P Series accuracy (±0.3% for P250) is comparable to high-quality fixed-mount instruments. The difference is repeatability — a fixed-mount unit maintains consistent angle and distance to the target. With a handheld, operator technique affects repeatability. Steady the instrument, confirm the spot via TTL sighting, and allow the response time to settle before reading. For continuous process monitoring, a fixed-mount unit is more appropriate. The portable is for spot checks, commissioning, and maintenance rounds.
